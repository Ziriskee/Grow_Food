Fluted Pumpkin

Telfairia occidentalis Hook.f.

  • Ugu · Igbo
  • Ugwu · Igbo

A vigorous climbing vine grown for iron-rich dark leaves and protein-rich seeds. It is one of Nigeria's most widely eaten indigenous vegetables, and the seeds are milled into egusi. It is dioecious, so some vines never bear pods.

  • Rainfed, staked100 × 100 cm
  • Dry season, valley bottom, unstaked50 × 30 cm
  • Soil pH6.0–7.5

Climate and soil

Grown all year. The rainfed crop runs roughly March to November; the dry-season crop runs November to March under irrigation, or on residual moisture in valley bottoms.

Moist, well-drained, fertile soil; responds strongly to organic matter.

NIHORT 2020

Planting

Sow in beds under shade or in nursery trays, 30-50 cm between rows on beds or 10-15 cm in trays. Transplant at the 6-leaf stage, when the vine is about 70 mm.

Seed rate is 10,000-20,000 seeds per hectare depending on target plant density. The dry-season system is a different crop technically: dense, unstaked, short-cycle.

NIHORT 2020

Water and nutrition

Rainfed crop depends on steady rainfall. Dry-season production uses irrigation, residual moisture in valley bottoms, or streams. Do not assume the crop needs twice-weekly watering in all systems; that depends on soil and season.

Apply about 20 t/ha of poultry manure before planting. Where that is not practical, apply 25-50% pre-plant and supply the rest with a split NPK top-dress. Two split applications of 80-90 kg N/ha have given about 10 t/ha of vine. These are per-hectare figures: scale to the plot, and do not copy them to a 0.1 ha garden.

NIHORT 2020

Pests and diseases

Before any spray

No pesticide is recommended on this page. If a pest problem justifies a chemical, use only a product registered by NAFDAC for that crop on the date of use, follow the label dose, wear the stated PPE, and observe the pre-harvest interval. Re-check registration before every season.

  • Banned or phased out: Carbofuran (Furadan), paraquat, atrazine and chlorpyrifos are banned or phased out by NAFDAC. Banned products still circulate in markets, so buy only from a licensed dealer and check the label.
  • PPE: Long sleeves, trousers, gloves, boots, eye protection and a nose/mouth cover when handling or spraying any agrochemical. Wash work clothes separately and bathe after spraying.
  • Pre-harvest interval: Follow the product label. Never harvest leaves inside the stated interval; leafy crops are eaten raw or lightly cooked and residues are not removed by washing.
  • Water warning: A 2022 Okigwe survey found 26.7% of dry-season growers irrigating with wastewater. That is a food-safety risk for a leaf crop eaten fresh; do not present it as a recommendation.

Start with staking, clean seed, rotation and sanitation. The old guide's chemical list is not a safe default: check every active ingredient against NAFDAC before publication, and prefer cultural control on a small plot.

White leaf spot disease

Stake early so leaves stay off the soil, remove badly infected leaves, avoid overhead watering, and rotate away from cucurbits.

NIHORT 2020

Root-knot nematodes pest

Use healthy seed, rotate with cereals, add organic matter, and destroy infected roots. NIHORT's guide lists carbofuran (Furadan) for this; it is banned in Nigeria and must not be used.

NIHORT 2020 · NAFDAC 2023

Harvest and after

Cut vines with a clean knife about 5 cm above a node so the plant can sprout again; leave enough leaves to support regrowth. Harvest in the early morning or late evening while plants are turgid. Pods mature 90-115 days after flowering; seed kept in the pod stays viable 3-5 months, or about 3 weeks in the lower fridge compartment.

Keep bundles cool and damp, out of the sun, and transport under the same conditions. Do not wash before storage. Harvest only what you can sell or eat within a few days; leafy ugu wilts fast.

NIHORT 2020

What we know, and how well

  • extensionB

    The rainfed crop is planted with the rains (roughly March-November); dry-season ugu is grown November-March under irrigation or in valley bottoms.

    NIHORT 2020
  • extensionB

    Stake after the first cut, about 4-6 weeks after planting, and drive stakes at least 30 cm into the soil. Staking keeps leaves off the soil, reduces white leaf spot, and makes harvesting easier.

    NIHORT 2020
  • extensionA

    Do not treat scattered early rains in southern Nigeria as the start of the season. NiMet's 2026 forecast warns that early rains are not onset and gives state-level onset dates.

    2026 season, national forecast

    NiMet 2026
  • researchC

    Most smallholder ugu in the southeast is grown by women on farms averaging about 0.7 ha, and the crop is profitable but inputs are used inefficiently: labour, seed and manure tend to be over-applied while land and capital are under-used.

    n = 400 farms, southeast Nigeria, 2023 study

    JAAT 2023
  • researchC

    In Enugu, 63% of ugu farmers had no extension contact in the previous year, and most sold through village or local markets (56.9%), village squares (27.6%) and roadside stands (16.2%); only 3.6% sold through cooperatives.

    n = 160 farmers, Enugu State, 2013 survey

    J. Agric. Extension 2013
  • researchC

    Dry-season ugu in Okigwe was profitable in 2021/22, but growers depended on streams and wastewater for irrigation and faced high input costs, poor roads and no irrigation facilities.

    Okigwe, Imo State, 2022 profitability study

    JAAT 2022
  • editorialA

    NIHORT's ugu guide still recommends Furadan (carbofuran) for nematodes. NAFDAC banned carbofuran outright in 2023, so that section of the guide must not be copied.

    NIHORT 2020 · NAFDAC 2023

Open questions

These are the things this page cannot yet answer. They are next season's field work.

  1. Do southeast growers cut at 5 cm above a node, or leave 30 cm as the old GRO page said? Measure regrowth on both in the 2027 season.
  2. Which staking method (single stake, tripod, trellis, table) gives the best labour return at 0.25-0.5 ha?
  3. How much does bamboo stake replacement actually cost per season, and is there a cheaper local material?
  4. Does wastewater irrigation change leaf contamination levels enough to justify a public warning beyond the survey finding?
  5. Which local seed sources give reliable germination, and what is the real cost per 1,000 viable seeds?
